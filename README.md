# The-Village-That-Looks-Back

A browser-based survival puzzle game that teaches quantum mechanics through play.

You arrive at a strange village where statues, windows, lanterns and a crossing behave according to quantum rules. Instead of answering a quiz, you learn the physics by interacting with the world.

## What the game teaches

The game turns quantum ideas into physical game mechanics:

- **Superposition** — a quantum state can hold multiple possibilities before measurement.
- **Measurement / collapse** — observing a state produces a definite result.
- **Basis switching** — the same quantum state can be measured using different lenses.
- **Measurement incompatibility** — changing the measurement basis changes what can be known with certainty.
- **Projection** — measurement projects the state onto the observed outcome.
- **Repeated measurement** — once a state has collapsed into a basis, measuring again in that same basis gives the same result.
- **Hadamard transformation** — the game's “Dusk” lens changes the measurement basis.
- **Entanglement** — the Twin Lanterns are bound together and their correlated outcomes can be tested.
- **Measurement order** — looking at a state in the middle of a sequence can destroy an otherwise deterministic result.
- **Quantum interference / circuit ordering** — the Mirror Crossing demonstrates that the order of quantum operations matters.

## Gameplay

You have three hearts. Each level gives you a physical puzzle rather than a conventional question.

### Level 1 — The Square of Statues

**Goal:** ring the bell when all three statues are turned away.

You can:

- photograph a statue to measure it;
- spin its vane to apply a transformation;
- use repeated measurement to learn what the state has become.

This level introduces measurement, state collapse, basis choice and deterministic vs probabilistic outcomes.

### Level 2 — Windows That Remember

**Goal:** discover the four-symbol door code.

Each window contains a hidden figure. Some windows have candles, indicating that the Dusk lens is useful.

You must decide which lens to use and record the observed results before entering the code.

This level demonstrates that measurement results depend on the basis used.

### Level 3 — The Twin Lanterns

**Goal:** make the two lanterns agree three times in a row.

First bind the lanterns at the shrine. Then choose the lens for each shore and photograph them.

The level demonstrates:

- entanglement;
- correlated measurement results;
- same-basis agreement;
- different-basis behavior;
- the importance of performing the right preparation before measurement.

### Level 4 — The Mirror Crossing

**Goal:** build a sequence that makes the boat reach the far shore on all three crossings.

Available operations:

- **Mirror** — a Hadamard-like transformation;
- **Fog bell** — a phase operation;
- **Look back** — measurement;
- **Rusted vane** — a distraction that does nothing.

The important lesson is that quantum operations do not generally commute: changing the order can change the final measurement.
